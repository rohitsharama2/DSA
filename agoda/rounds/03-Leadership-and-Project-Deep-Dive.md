# Agoda Staff: project depth and leadership

[Round-wise index](README.md) · [Platform workbook](02-Platform-and-System-Design.md)

Agoda's [official interview guide](https://careersatagoda.com/interview) describes Context–Action–Results (CAR) for competency evidence. Prepare real examples with explicit personal contribution. The prompts and evaluation targets here are our preparation recommendations, not a published Staff rubric.

## Two project briefs

Choose one architecture/delivery project and one reliability/migration project. For each, write a one-page brief covering the customer problem, your scope, constraints, baseline, alternatives, chosen design, personal decisions, rollout, observed results, and lessons. Distinguish measured results from estimates and team work from your contribution.

Rehearse a two-minute overview and a fifteen-minute deep dive. Be able to answer why the simpler option did not suffice, which decision you reversed, and what evidence would make you redesign it today.

| Prompt | Evidence to include | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You influenced another team without authority | Shared problem, their concerns, decision mechanism, adoption | What happened when they disagreed? |
| You pushed back on a design | Constraint, credible alternatives, evidence, final ownership | How did you support a decision you opposed? |
| A production incident exposed a design weakness | Detection, containment, communication, durable repair | Which action reduced recurrence rather than just symptoms? |
| You mentored an engineer | Specific skill gap, feedback, growing autonomy | What could they later do without you? |
| You delivered under an unclear deadline | Scope negotiation, risk reduction, checkpoints | What did you deliberately postpone and why? |
| Your decision failed | Your assumption, impact, correction, changed practice | How did you communicate accountability? |

## Answer scaffold

“The problem was [specific context]. I owned [scope] while [other team] owned [boundary]. We considered [alternatives]. I chose/proposed [decision] because [evidence]. I aligned [stakeholders] through [action], rolled it out using [steps], and measured [result]. The limitation was [fact]; next time I would [change].”

Use this structure with your own facts; do not invent scale, savings, or leadership responsibilities. A credible smaller example with clear judgment is more useful than an unsupported large claim.

## Questions for the hiring manager

- What does successful Staff ownership look like after six months on this team?
- Which decisions belong to Staff engineers, Leads, and Engineering Managers here?
- What reliability, latency, or delivery problem needs attention first?
- How are cross-team architecture decisions agreed and revisited?
- How much of the role is implementation, platform work, mentoring, and coordination?

**Readiness:** Six distinct stories, two deep dives, clear personal contribution, and at least one failure/lesson in each project. Match the role's actual scope rather than importing another company's Staff expectations.
