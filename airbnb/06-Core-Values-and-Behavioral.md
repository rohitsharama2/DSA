# Phase 6 — Core values, collaboration, and project depth

**Next preparation:** [Hiring-manager expectations and answer practice](rounds/04-Hiring-Manager-Questions-and-Answers.md).

**Reference:** [Concepts and worked solutions](../handbook/DSA-CS-AI-Concepts-and-Solutions.md). Section numbers below refer to this guide.

**Time:** Week 10, with two short story-practice sessions every week from phase 0 onward.

Airbnb lists four core values: **Champion the Mission**, **Be a Host**, **Embrace the Adventure**, and **Be a Cereal Entrepreneur**. Read the official descriptions before selecting examples. [Life at Airbnb](https://careers.airbnb.com/life-at-airbnb/)

The prompts below are suggested preparation exercises, not official interview questions.

| Value | Suggested evidence to prepare | Practice prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Champion the Mission | A decision that considered community or user impact | When did understanding a user's experience change your decision? |
| Be a Host | Listening, inclusion, support, and constructive collaboration | When did you help someone participate or succeed? |
| Embrace the Adventure | Learning, adaptation, and honest reflection | How did you respond when an important plan failed? |
| Be a Cereal Entrepreneur | Initiative and creative use of constraints | What did you accomplish with limited time or resources? |

### Prepare eight truthful stories

- [ ] A difficult decision with a real trade-off.
- [ ] A failure and what changed afterward.
- [ ] A disagreement handled constructively.
- [ ] Helping or mentoring someone.
- [ ] Understanding and addressing a user problem.
- [ ] Taking ownership beyond an assigned task.
- [ ] Delivering under constraints.
- [ ] Learning something unfamiliar and applying it.

Map each story to relevant values without forcing the connection. Use **Situation → Task → Action → Result → Reflection**. State what you personally did, what others contributed, and what you would do differently. Include measured outcomes when available; never invent metrics. Personal examples are useful when you are comfortable sharing them.

### Prepare two project deep dives

For each project, write one page covering the problem, users, scope, architecture, your contribution, alternatives rejected, hardest failure, delivery decisions, outcome, and lessons. Prepare both a two-minute overview and a ten-minute technical discussion.

Practice explaining the same trade-off to an engineer and to a product manager. For example: why a booking correctness issue can justify postponing a feature, what evidence supports the decision, and how you would reduce the delay.

### Prepare “Why Airbnb?”

Connect a specific product or community observation to your motivation and relevant experience. Explain how the advertised role would let you contribute. Use your actual experience; do not imply that you have hosted or booked through Airbnb if you have not.

**Readiness check:** Tell four different stories clearly in 2–3 minutes each, then handle follow-up questions without changing the facts. Explain both projects at technical and nontechnical depth. Make your answers understandable to someone who has not read your resume.

## Community contribution and volunteering prompt

The [supplied candidate report](Interview-Experience-and-Question-Tracker.md) specifically mentions a question about community contributions or volunteer experiences. Add one honest community-focused story to your bank.

Possible examples include mentoring peers, maintaining an open-source project, organizing a study group, improving accessibility, helping a local group, or formal volunteering. Choose an actual experience and describe its scope accurately. If you have no formal volunteering experience, say so and discuss a relevant contribution you genuinely made.

Practice these prompts:

- What community did you contribute to, and what did its members need?
- What did you personally do, and how did you listen to others?
- What changed, what did you learn, and what would you do differently?
- Did your contribution continue beyond the initial activity?

Avoid inventing an activity to fit a value. A modest, specific example with reflection is more useful preparation than a grand but unsupported claim.
