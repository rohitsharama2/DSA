## 3. Three project summaries to prepare

Use **problem → your responsibility → one decision → result**. Start with 45–60 seconds; expand to two minutes only when invited. Resume facts support the drafts below, but do not supply every architectural choice, team boundary or measurement detail.

### A. Ushur: substantial ownership and business impact

**Best for:** “What have you owned?”, “What is your strongest project?”, “Why senior scope?”

> At Ushur, I led full-stack development of Self-Serve Insights on Apache Superset as part of migrating legacy analytics. My work covered the analytics product, and I separately owned development and deployment of a Python/FastAPI transformation service using dbt. The migration's estimated annual licensing and compute savings were $400,000 to $500,000. For a deeper discussion, I can walk through my specific responsibilities, the migration decisions and the basis of that estimate.

**Complete before using as a two-minute story:** What was the original limitation? Which decision did you personally own? Who selected Superset? Which alternatives were evaluated? What migrated, and what was still pending? Who collaborated with you? What was the rollout plan? Avoid implying the transformation service was on the migration's critical path unless that is true.

**Expected probes:** “Did you lead people or the technical work?” “Who approved the design?” “Were the savings realized?” “How many users or teams?” Answer with your actual scope. A useful estimate explanation is old annual licensing/compute cost minus replacement and ongoing operating costs; establish what your estimate actually included and who reviewed it.

**Short boundary answer:** “That figure was an estimate of annual savings, not a statement of booked savings.” Add actual rollout and validation status.

### B. Luckd: backend correctness and end-to-end delivery

**Best for:** “What is a complex system you built?”, “How much backend work have you done?”, “What does end-to-end ownership mean for you?”

> At Luckd, I built customer and partner React Native apps, a React operations portal, and TypeScript/Node.js APIs backed by PostgreSQL. My work included payment verification, webhook handling and delivery reconciliation. A key part was handling duplicate callbacks and uncertain transaction outcomes, using concurrency controls, idempotency and recovery flows. I also centralized order states across the apps, backend and operational tools and handled releases across those surfaces.

**Prepare one real trace:** payment initiated → provider result/webhook → verification → persisted state → reconciliation if the outcome is uncertain. Name the real idempotency key, concurrency mechanism and retry/recovery behavior you implemented. The resume does not establish whether these were row locks, uniqueness constraints, queues, or another design; do not borrow a textbook mechanism and present it as your implementation.

**Expected probes:** “What happens if a callback arrives twice?” “What was your personal contribution?” “Was this a full-time role?” “How does it overlap with CoverSelf?” “What is your ongoing commitment?” Prepare factual engagement dates/type and availability. “Founding Engineer” does not by itself establish company ownership, funding, employees or full-time employment.

**Scope boundary:** This supports payment integration and workflow correctness. It does not establish building a payment processor, distributed transaction engine, or Google-scale infrastructure. No transaction-volume or reliability percentage is supplied; describe failure cases handled instead of inventing metrics.

### C. CoverSelf: current work and user impact

**Best for:** “What are you doing now?”, “How have you used AI?”, “What impact have you had recently?”

> At CoverSelf, I built AI-assisted claims and policy workflows, including conversational claims-rule authoring, policy Q&A and authoring tools. The authoring workflow reduced authoring time by 85%. I also implemented frontend caching with an estimated 30% reduction in API requests, added IndexedDB persistence and browser-side data analysis, and worked on reusable UI components and rendering issues.

**Choose one outcome for the short answer.** Be ready with the authoring task, before/after time, sample, measurement period and whether review/correction time was included. An 85% reduction means the new time is 15% of the baseline; it is not an accuracy score. Caching's estimated 30% reduction concerns API requests, not cost savings or latency.

**Expected probes:** “Who used this?” “Was it production or a pilot?” “What did the model do versus your application?” “How were incorrect outputs handled?” “What did you own?” The available notes do not establish rollout status or scale for every feature. Confirm each rather than grouping them all as production deployments.

**AI depth answer:** “My AI experience is in application workflows and integrations. At Ushur I also built an AI analyst POC using LangGraph/Mastra, a React orchestration UI and custom Superset MCP servers.” Keep the POC label; no model-training or ML-research experience is established by these facts.

> “Could you share the budgeted compensation range and its breakdown for this role and location? I'd like to understand fixed pay, target bonus and equity before giving a precise expectation.”

If asked for current compensation, have an accurate breakdown ready: fixed annual cash `[ ]`; target/variable pay `[ ]`; equity type and vesting `[ ]`; one-time payments `[ ]`. ## 5. Questions to ask the recruiter

Prioritize the first four if time is short, then resolve remaining logistics by email or the next conversation.

1. “Which requisition, team or product is this for, and is the scope full-stack, frontend or broader SWE?”
2. “Is the opening targeted at L5, and how is level evaluated through the process?”
3. “What interviews or assessments are planned, including coding, design, leadership or frontend-specific content?”
4. “Can I use JavaScript or TypeScript throughout the coding interviews? What runtime, libraries and editor are provided?”
5. “What is the likely scheduling timeline, and what preparation resources would you recommend for this particular role?”
6. “What location and office-attendance expectations apply?”
7. “What is the budgeted compensation range and component structure?”
8. “Is team matching part of this opening, and at what point are the team and offer finalized?”

**Close in one sentence:** “From what we've discussed, the strongest match seems to be [scope]; is there any part of my background you'd like me to clarify before the next step?” Record the actual next step, owner and expected follow-up date.

Afterward score five areas **0 = missing, 1 = vague, 2 = specific and defensible**: concise introduction, personal ownership, outcome evidence, honest role fit, and logistics readiness. This is a coaching score, not a hiring predictor. Repair the lowest-scoring answer and repeat it once; don't memorize ten alternate scripts.
