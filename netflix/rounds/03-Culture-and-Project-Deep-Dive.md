# Netflix: culture, leadership, and project depth

[Round-wise index](README.md) · [Design workbook](02-System-Design-and-Data-Modeling.md)

Read the current [official culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture). It emphasizes effective colleagues, responsible autonomy, ambitious work, and continual improvement, with candid feedback and informed decision ownership. Use it to understand the working environment. It is not a script to recite or a substitute for truthful evidence.

The questions below are our practice prompts, not Netflix's official rubric. N1/N5 and the Ads summaries provide context for project/collaboration discussion; level and team differences remain in the [source register](Research-Sources.md).

## Build two project dossiers

Pick one significant technical decision and one cross-team delivery or recovery effort. For each, record the problem, business/user context, your authority, unknowns, alternatives, decision evidence, collaborators, implementation, operational behavior, rollout, measured outcome, and what you would change.

Rehearse a two-minute introduction, a ten-minute technical walkthrough, and unstructured follow-ups. Be ready to defend real data models, failure cases, estimates, and design choices rather than presenting a generic architecture.

| Practice question | What makes the answer concrete | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When did you make a difficult decision with incomplete information? | Decision owner, missing facts, reversible steps, risk limits | What new evidence would reverse it? |
| Describe feedback you gave that was hard to hear | Specific behavior, private/respectful delivery, listening, outcome | How did you check that it helped? |
| What feedback changed your own behavior? | Initial reaction, action, sustained change | What remains difficult? |
| When did you disagree with the chosen direction? | Strongest opposing case, evidence, decision process, support afterward | Did you surface the concern early enough? |
| How did you improve a system beyond your immediate task? | Actual user/operational problem, coordination, result | How did you avoid unowned scope expansion? |
| Tell me about a failed bet | Your judgment, impact, response, lesson | How did you distinguish bad luck from a flawed assumption? |

Do not equate candor with bluntness or autonomy with bypassing partners. Explain how you supplied context, invited dissent, made a decision, and remained accountable for its consequences.

## Senior / Staff extension

If the requisition confirms broader scope, add a story about influencing architecture across teams and developing other engineers' judgment. Explain what you personally owned and what you enabled others to own. Without a confirmed level, do not infer scope from years of experience alone.

For Ads roles, connect relevant experience to budgets, delivery measurement, latency, or data contracts only where you have it. For playback/platform/UI roles, choose corresponding evidence. Acknowledge gaps and identify transferable decisions rather than inventing domain expertise.

## Questions to ask the team

- Which decisions would I own, and where would I need to coordinate with partners?
- What technical/domain expertise is essential on day one versus learnable here?
- What is the hardest current reliability, product, or data-quality trade-off?
- How does the team give feedback and revisit decisions after new evidence?
- What concrete outcomes would demonstrate success at this level in six months?

**Readiness:** Two defensible project dossiers, six stories with distinct evidence, a failure you can analyze honestly, and thoughtful questions tied to the actual team.
