# Stripe: project depth and leadership

[Round-wise index](README.md) · [Design workbook](03-System-Design-and-Followups.md)

Stripe's [careers material and operating principles](https://stripe.com/careers/compatibility) provide company context around users, craft, urgency, and collaboration. The prompts below are our preparation framework, not an official assessment rubric.

## Two project briefs

Prepare one user-facing delivery project and one reliability/integration project. Write: user problem, baseline evidence, your role, constraints, alternatives, decision, implementation, rollout, results, and lessons. Rehearse a two-minute overview and a fifteen-minute technical deep dive.

| Practice question | Evidence to include | Follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When did user feedback change your design? | Actual feedback, original assumption, revised behavior, observed result | How did you separate one request from a general need? |
| How did you move quickly without compromising correctness? | Risk boundary, scoped first release, tests, rollback | Which shortcut did you refuse and why? |
| What was your hardest integration/debugging problem? | Reproduction, hypotheses, decisive evidence, cause-level fix | What prevented recurrence? |
| Describe disagreement with a partner team | Their constraints, alternatives, decision mechanism, relationship afterward | What did you learn from their view? |
| How did you raise engineering quality? | Specific review, tooling, mentoring, or API improvement | What measurable outcome changed? |
| Tell me about a decision that failed | Your responsibility, impact, repair, changed practice | What did you communicate before knowing the full cause? |

For senior candidates, add cross-team scope, ownership boundaries, prioritization, and how others became more effective. Do not invent Staff-level authority to fit a title that has not been confirmed.

## Answer scaffold

“The user problem was [fact]. I owned [scope] and worked with [partners]. We considered [alternatives]; I chose/proposed [decision] because [evidence]. We reduced risk through [steps]. The measured outcome was [result], with [attribution limit]. I would change [lesson] next time.”

Use real numbers when known and label estimates. Separate your decisions from the team's combined achievements. Prepare the failure path and rollout details, not only a success narrative.

## Questions for the team

- Which user or developer experience needs the most improvement now?
- What correctness/reliability decisions would this role own?
- How do teams negotiate API changes and migration responsibilities?
- What distinguishes success at this exact level after six months?
- Which production or integration problems consume the most engineering time?

**Readiness:** Two defensible briefs, six distinct stories, and the ability to explain one hard technical trade-off without relying on polished wording.
